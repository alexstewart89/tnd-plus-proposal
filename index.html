1. What Shopify Plus costs at your scale

Shopify Plus has one platform fee, assessed monthly. You pay the fixed monthly minimum, or a percentage of that month's online sales through Shopify, whichever is higher. On a 3-year term the minimum is £1,800 a month and the percentage is 0.35%. Below about £514k of monthly sales (around £6.2M a year) you pay the minimum. Above it, the percentage applies to the whole month's volume. There are no setup fees, no hosting fees and no version upgrade fees.

How to read it. At £5M of online sales the percentage works out below the minimum, so you simply pay £1,800 a month. From roughly £6.2M a year upwards the fee becomes 0.35% of sales and scales with you. At £30M it is £105,000 a year, still 0.35%. Because it is assessed month by month, quieter months fall back to the £1,800 minimum and busier months are charged at 0.35%, so the annual figure for an uneven year sits slightly above the flat percentage shown.
Practitioner trade orders are charged at a lower rate. Orders placed through Shopify B2B company accounts (practitioners buying stock directly at trade pricing) attract a variable rate of 0.18% rather than 0.35%. For example, if £3M of a £10M year were practitioner trade orders through B2B, the platform fee would be £24,500 (D2C) plus £5,400 (B2B), £29,900 in total rather than £35,000. The exact split is confirmed in the Plus agreement.

"Online sales" means orders transacted through your Shopify store (online and B2B); orders imported from Odoo or other systems are excluded. Variable platform fees are subject to a monthly cap set out in the Plus agreement, which is not reached at any of the volumes above. All figures are for a 3-year Shopify Plus term.

4. Why move: the problems you described, and how Shopify answers them

Drawn from our previous calls in August. Each point starts with the problem in your words, then what changes on Shopify Plus.

"Most clients arrive on mobile, buy the basket their practitioner recommended and leave. Browsing anything else is hard enough that they don't."The journey today, as described on our previous calls

01Mobile first for the 80% who arrive on a phone

Today: a desktop-first site when most clients arrive from a practitioner's link on mobile.

Every Shopify theme is built mobile first, and the storefront is served from Shopify's global CDN. Clients who land from a practitioner recommendation get a fast page, a basket that is easy to add to, and a checkout that works with a thumb. This is where the lost cross-sell lives: a client who can browse comfortably buys the second and third product.

Plus-wide: included in every theme and in Shopify Checkout.

02Your team changes the site. Not an IT ticket.

Today: every change is coded by in-house IT. A banner can take three months.

Banners, landing pages, brand pages, education content, product merchandising and promotions are all done in the theme editor by the commercial and marketing team, the same day. Launchpad schedules campaigns in advance, and Flow automates the repetitive work. Developers are reserved for the things that genuinely need them: the practitioner app and the Odoo integration.

Replaces: the IT backlog for day-to-day trading changes.

03A checkout clients trust

Today: a checkout that "doesn't inspire trust" for a healthcare purchase.

Shopify Checkout is the most used checkout on the internet, and an independent study found it converts on average 15% better than other platforms, up to 36%. Shop Pay lifts conversion by as much as 50% versus guest checkout because the client's details are already saved. For a business whose traffic is referred rather than browsed, every point of checkout conversion lands straight on revenue.

Included with Shopify Payments, no extra cost.

04Turn the practitioner's basket into a bigger order

Today: clients buy exactly what was recommended and leave, unaware that the same products they buy elsewhere are cheaper here.

The practitioner's basket becomes the starting point rather than the whole visit. Complementary products are recommended on the product page and in the basket, a free-delivery threshold nudges the order up, and a one-click offer after payment adds an item without re-entering card details. Repeat protocols can be set up as subscriptions so the second, third and fourth orders happen without the practitioner having to prompt them. Everything here is configured by your team in the admin, not built by developers, and because the practitioner stays linked to the client, commission follows the larger order too.

Direct impact on average order value and repeat rate, which today are capped by the journey rather than by demand.

05Discounts attached to the client, not codes flying around

Today: practitioners hand out discount codes that get shared, misused and break commission attribution.

On Shopify the discount lives on the client's account. A practitioner allocates a percentage to a specific client in their portal, and it is applied automatically at checkout for that client only, with Shopify Functions enforcing the rule. No codes to share, and every order carries the practitioner link that commission depends on.

Confirmed: the approach proposed by the Shopify partners you have seen pitch.

06Practitioner-only brands stay out of sight

Today: complex visibility rules for high-strength brands that the public must not see.

Customer segments and catalogues control who sees what. A credentialed practitioner sees the full range at trade pricing; a client sees the public range plus whatever their practitioner has added to their basket; a future D2C shopper sees the consumer range only. Products that are restricted never appear in search, collections or Google for anyone not entitled to them.

Confirmed: on our previous calls.

07The practitioner portal, without going headless

Today: an out-of-the-box B2B portal does not fit a practitioner-to-client model.

The model we agreed in August: Shopify B2B handles practitioner trade purchasing as standard, and a custom app embedded in the Shopify customer account handles protocols, recommended baskets, client allocation and the practitioner's own sales and commission view, pulling commission data from Odoo through your Core middleware. One login, one account, no headless front end to maintain. Your agency builds the app once and you own it.

Validated with Shopify's solutions engineering team and independently proposed by the agencies pitching.

08Practitioner onboarding from 25 steps to one form

Today: a 25-step manual onboarding process with certificates checked by hand.

Shopify Forms captures the application and the qualification documents; Flow routes it for approval, tags the account on sign-off and unlocks the practitioner catalogue automatically. Verification against professional registers can be added by the agency as a Flow step later.

Included in Plus.

09Odoo stays the single source of truth

Today: Odoo went live on 31 July and the new platform must not destabilise it.

Shopify has established Odoo connectors for products, stock, orders and customers, and the Plus API limits (up to 500% higher than standard plans) give the Core middleware the headroom to sync 18,000 SKUs across 650 brands without throttling. Shopify captures the sale and the discount; Odoo does the rest.

Confirmed: on our previous calls.

10SEO and AI search from day one

Today: no SEO, no paid media, and a historical view that showing up in Google would be a failure.

Shopify storefronts ship with structured data, clean URLs, fast rendering and editable metadata on every page, so the new commercial team can start on organic growth immediately. Shopify is also building the plumbing for AI shopping agents, with products discoverable and purchasable in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot, so the catalogue is ready for where search is heading without another build.

Enables the growth strategy the current platform never allowed.

11Ireland, the EU and a D2C brand without a second platform

Today: an Irish entity, EU ambitions and a possible D2C brand, with the risk of disconnected systems.

Shopify Markets sells into Ireland and the EU in local currency from the same catalogue, and multi-entity lets the Irish company settle to its own bank account under one store. A separate D2C brand can run as an expansion store (included) sharing the same catalogue and Odoo connection, so it never competes for the practitioner network's attention.

Plus-only multi-entity and expansion stores.

12Diagnostics, with the right boundary

Today: a planned lab-testing service with sensitive health data, consent and GDPR to get right.

The pattern that works, and the one we would recommend: Shopify sells the test kit and holds the order and a kit reference, while results, interpretations and consent live in a clinically compliant partner portal that practitioners and clients reach from their account. Shopify is not designed to store health records and we would not propose it does. This keeps the commerce launch and the diagnostics launch on separate, realistic timelines (you suggested Q1 2027 for diagnostics) without compromising either.

Honest scope: we will put the data boundary in writing with your chosen agency before build.
